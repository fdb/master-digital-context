# Master Digital Context

This is the course program for the master in the digital context, a master programme at [Sint Lucas Antwerpen](https://www.sintlucasantwerpen.be/).

This is a one-year master programme around digital media, and the critical intersection of technology and society. A one-year premaster programme introduces students from other bachelors about our approach of working.

## Vision of the Master in the Digital Context

Combining technology and design, the Master in Digital Media is a breeding ground for innovative ideas and groundbreaking projects in the field of art, design and physical / digital computing. It looks at technology not just as a creative tool, but as an entirely different medium with its own guidelines for design and storytelling. The master aims to balance the technical, artistic and creative aspects of digital media, focusing on giving learners the knowledge and insights to hone their own tools. We promote work that ranges from the imminently practical to the whimsical, artistic and creative. We enable students to build their own languages of expressions through mastery of digital media.

## Goal of the Masterproject

This course aims to teach you the tools, technologies, and methodologies you need to work in the digital medium. We encourage you to interpret our environment artistically through code. You learn to visualize and design digitally from the ever-changing data world around us. You'll gain insight into the black box of programming and artificial intelligence.

The context is divided into several themes:

- digital media: the use of code, procedures, and algorithms to generate new designs. Here we look at the importance of interaction, 2D / 3D / VR, and installation art.
- data visualization: making information understandable through visualizations. Here we learn to deal with all aspects of data: from collecting, filtering, and mining to visualizing. We also look at storytelling: how you convey a story with data is at least as important as the data itself.
- artificial creativity: based on machine learning and AI we learn the computer how to design and make art.

### Learning objectives

- Relate to multi-dimensionality, complexity and unpredictability in a digital context
- You question your own position and relate your practice to its broader societal context.
- You broaden your understanding by engaging diverse artistic and design frameworks.
- You recognize a diversity and simultaneity of equal perspectives and practices. You acknowledge that 'value' is produced in context.
- You integrate evolving insights into your research, reframing it without abandoning it.

### Independence in research of your project in a digital context

- You formulate your research question in a clear, focused, and open way.
- You carry out your research independently.
- You critically reflect on your own work and engage with feedback on your own terms, deliberately choosing what to integrate and what to set aside.

### Artistic and methodological quality in the development and elaboration of your project in a digital context

- Your methodology is your own artistic language; you develop and document your methods in detail as a manual for your artistic process.
- You experiment systematically throughout your research, implementing innovative or tailor-made methods using digital media concepts (code, data, algorithms, machine learning).
- You identify and apply the values and skills characteristic of working, learning, and investigating in a digital context.
- Your project presented at the jury demonstrates clear artistic and methodological quality. Markers include but are not limited to: craft and execution, coherence between concept and form, and how thoughtfully its technical, aesthetic, and narrative aspects work together.

### Taking final responsibility for your own project

- You take final responsibility for the process, the outcome and the possible continuity of your project.
- You take charge in planning, solving problems, mediating, adjusting, and presenting your work.
- You critically reflect on your own work and engage thoughtfully with feedback, integrating it on your own terms.

### Qualitative communication about your work and process

- You apply qualitative communication at every stage of your process.
- You document that process as a processfolio for the jury.
- You present your work clearly and comprehensibly to peers.
- You tailor your story to each audience (peers, professors, academic, professional).
- You situate your work in relation to other practices and the broader digital field.
